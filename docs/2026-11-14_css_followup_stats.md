# CSS follow-up statistics — for the NHK 2024 YR4 documentary

**Date:** 2026-11-14 · **For:** Greg (NHK filming, Mon–Tue) · **From:** Rob

All numbers come from our live replica of the MPC database (`mpc_sbn` on
Gizmo), queried 2026-11-14. "NEO" means q ≤ 1.30 AU on MPC's current orbit.
Times are UTC.

## Headline numbers

1. **About 126 million observations.** That is how much the CSS telescopes
   have put into MPC's database, about **22% of every observation of every
   observer in history** (~562 million).
2. **The first follow-up of 2024 YR4 by anyone came from CSS.** The
   Mt. Lemmon 1.0 m (I52) observed it **35 minutes after it was posted to
   the NEO Confirmation Page**. Within the same half hour, CSS found it in
   Catalina survey images from the night *before* ATLAS discovered it.
   Timeline below.
3. **In 2025 the CSS follow-up telescopes observed 69% of all newly
   discovered NEOs** (2,224 of 3,249), and **58% of those found by other
   surveys** (1,369 of 2,368).
4. **I52 is the world's #1 non-survey NEO observatory by number of
   different NEOs observed:** 2,293 in 2025, ahead of Cerro Tololo (807,
   1,839) and Westfield ARO (H21, 1,813). It is #1 again in 2026 so far.
5. **About 847,000 NEO observations from CSS overall.** CSS has supplied
   17–27% of the world's NEO observations every year since 2017.

## CSS's share of the world's follow-up

How large the share looks depends on what you count:

| Measure (2025) | CSS follow-up (I52+V06+G84) |
|---|---|
| NEOs discovered in 2025 that CSS followed up | **69%** of all; **58%** of other surveys' discoveries |
| Different NEOs observed, rank among non-survey stations | **#1** (I52) |
| Different NEOs observed, vs. all observers | 2,714 of 7,036 (**39%**) |
| NEO *observations*, share of non-survey stations | 12.6% |

Two years compared with the peak:

| Discovery year | NEOs | % followed by CSS follow-up | % of other surveys' NEOs followed | CSS first to follow up (others' NEOs) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2020 (peak) | 2,979 | 85% | 74% | 15% |
| 2025 | 3,249 | 69% | 58% | 12% |

**"We dwarf all other stations combined" doesn't hold as stated.** In 2025
all other non-survey stations together observed 5,548 different NEOs, about
twice CSS follow-up's 2,714, and they submitted about 7× as many NEO
observations. The claims that do hold:
- **CSS follow-up is the largest single non-survey NEO follow-up effort.**
- **It reaches roughly two of every three new NEOs.**

The claim may come from an era when it was true, or from a narrower measure
such as NEOCP confirmations, which these tables don't isolate.

## Other "wow" candidates

- **CSS discovered about 318,000 minor planets** (by MPC's discovery flag,
  principal designations, all six CSS telescopes), plus 144 comets. MPC
  officially credits about 215,000 *numbered* minor planets to CSS sites.
- **As the first follow-up for other surveys' NEOs, I52 ranks #3 worldwide
  (2021–2025):** 716 objects. Only Pan-STARRS (F51) and Cerro Tololo (807)
  were first more often.

## Definitions and caveats

- **Follow-up telescopes:** I52, V06, G84.
- **"Survey" stations,** excluded from the non-survey comparisons:
  - CSS 703/E12/G96/693.
  - Pan-STARRS, ATLAS, Rubin, Bok (V00), NEOWISE.
  - LINEAR, NEAT, Spacewatch 691, LONEOS, ZTF (I41).
  - TESS (C57).
- **Coverage measure:** a CSS observation after discovery, within 200 days
  (the dashboard's apparition cache).
- **NEO statistics** use objects that are NEOs today. NEOCP candidates that
  turned out not to be NEOs are counted only in the all-object totals.
- **Denominators:** the 562 M total is PostgreSQL's row estimate for
  `obs_sbn`, not an exact count. The CSS counts are exact.
- **2026 is year-to-date** (through 14 Nov).
